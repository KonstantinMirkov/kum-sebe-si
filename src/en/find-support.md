## Psychologist, psychotherapist, psychiatrist: who is who?

People often do not know whom to turn to, and that alone stops many of them. Here is the simple version.

- A **psychologist** holds a university degree in psychology. They do counselling, psychological assessment, and help with specific difficulties and with learning how to cope.
- A **psychotherapist** has completed additional, extended training in a particular therapeutic approach and works with you over a longer period, usually in regular sessions. They are often psychologists or doctors.
- A **psychiatrist** is a medical doctor specialising in psychiatry. Only a doctor can make a medical diagnosis and prescribe medication.

They often work together. If you are not sure where to start, your family doctor or a consultation with a psychologist is a good first step. They can point you onward.

## How to choose a professional

A few things worth checking: what education and training they have, which approach they work in, whether they belong to a professional organisation and follow a code of ethics. Ask directly; a good professional will not take offence.

Recommendations from people you trust help, but they are no guarantee. The right person for your friend is not necessarily the right one for you. Be wary of promises of quick, guaranteed cures and of anyone who pressures you to decide on the spot.

## Questions you can ask

- What difficulties do you have experience with?
- How will sessions go and how often will we meet?
- What does a session cost and what happens if I cancel?
- How is the confidentiality of our conversations protected?
- How will we know that our work is helping?

## If the first step feels hard

That is normal. What has helped many people is booking an appointment at a moment when they feel relatively okay, asking a friend to come along, or writing a short message instead of calling. Also read [what a first meeting looks like](/en/articles/first-session/).

## If you are supporting someone else

You cannot make them seek help, but you can be there. Read more in [how to support someone close to you](/en/articles/supporting-someone/).
