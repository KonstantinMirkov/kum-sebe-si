A bit of anxiety is normal. It makes you prepare for the exam, double-check the keys before leaving, pay attention in an unfamiliar place. It becomes a problem when the alarm goes off without a real reason, does not quiet down for a long time and starts steering your days.

## What it can feel like from the inside

Some people describe anxiety as background noise that never switches off. Others feel it mostly in the body: a tight chest, a racing heart, a dry mouth, a knot in the stomach. A panic attack is the loudest version. Fear arrives all at once, sometimes out of nowhere, and it can feel like a heart attack. It usually settles within minutes, even if in the moment it does not feel that way.

Avoidance often joins in. You stop going to certain places, put off conversations, keep asking people close to you for reassurance. That brings quick relief, but it tends to feed the anxiety rather than calm it.

## What sometimes helps

A slow breath out, longer than the breath in. Naming a few things you can see around you. Less coffee, more sleep, a bit of movement. Telling someone “things have been hard lately”. It sounds small, but for many people it takes the edge off.

If anxiety keeps coming back, some kinds of therapy work well, cognitive behavioural therapy among them, and in some cases medication, which only a doctor can decide on.

## When not to wait

Anxious sensations can also have physical causes, such as thyroid problems. If you have chest pain or trouble breathing for the first time, see a doctor. In an emergency call **112**.
