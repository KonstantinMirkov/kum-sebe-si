In systemic therapy, a difficulty is not seen as something inside one person but as part of the web of relationships around them. No one is hunted down as the culprit. The question is how people affect each other and how they can start communicating better.

## Who comes

A family, a couple, parents with a teenager, or one person who talks about their relationships. Sometimes different members attend at different times.

## How sessions go

The therapist asks questions that help each person hear the other and see the situation from new angles. No side is taken. There are often exercises, such as how to hold a difficult conversation, how to share responsibilities, or what happens when everyone reacts in their usual way. Family history may be looked at if it connects to the present.

## When it is used

Conflicts, separation, a loss in the family, difficulties with children and teenagers, a new family, a close person's chronic illness. When someone faces something personal that is strongly tied to the people around them.

## What to ask

How is confidentiality handled when several people take part? What happens if someone does not want to participate? If there is violence in the family, safety comes first, before any joint work.

No approach is best for everyone. What suits you depends on you, on the difficulty and on the person sitting across from you. That is why it is best to talk it through with a psychologist or psychotherapist.
