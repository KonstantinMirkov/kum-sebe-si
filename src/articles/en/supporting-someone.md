When someone you love is hurting, the strongest urge is to fix things. But you rarely can. What you can do is be nearby.

## What often helps

Asking directly and without drama: “I have noticed you seem different lately. How are you, really?” And then listening, without rushing to solutions. Not brushing it off (“everyone goes through that”), not comparing, not saying “pull yourself together”. Admitting that it is hard. Sometimes “I am here” is enough.

Specific offers beat general ones. Instead of “tell me if you need anything”, try: “I can come over Thursday evening. Would you like that?”

## About a professional

You can suggest it, but you cannot force it. Help finding a professional, booking an appointment or going along often matters more than advice. If the answer is “no”, it is not the end. Stay, and try again later.

## Look after yourself too

Supporting someone is tiring. You need rest, your own people and limits. That is not selfish; it is what allows you to keep going.

## In case of danger

If the person talks about suicide, mentions harming themselves, or is in immediate danger, do not leave them alone and call **112**.
