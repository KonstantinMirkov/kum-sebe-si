We all get tired. Burnout is different: after a weekend, after a holiday, even after a few good nights, the feeling of running on empty comes back. The World Health Organization defines it as a syndrome resulting from chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed. It is not a diagnosis, but it is very real.

## Three things people often describe

Exhaustion that rest does not fix. A growing distance from work: you begin to get irritated with colleagues, think cynically, do only what is necessary. And a sense that you are no longer as good at what you do as you used to be.

These can be mistaken for depressive states or anxiety, and sometimes travel together with them, so only a professional can tell them apart.

## What usually helps

It is often worth looking at the workload first, not only at “self-care”. If you are doing the work of three people, a bath and a candle will not fix it. A conversation with a manager, redistributing tasks and clear limits on working hours can help. For you personally: sleep, movement, things that have nothing to do with being productive.

If you feel there is no way out, or physical complaints start to show up, see a doctor or psychologist. You do not have to hit rock bottom first.
