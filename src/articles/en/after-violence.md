If you have experienced violence, the first and most important thing you can hear is: it is not your fault. Whatever you did or did not do, the responsibility lies with the person who used violence.

## Safety first

If you are in immediate danger, call **112**. If you feel safe, think about whether you have somewhere to go and who should know where you are. If possible, see a doctor. Besides caring for your body, a medical examination and documentation can matter if you later decide on legal steps. You do not have to decide right away.

## Normal reactions

After violence it is common to feel shock, numbness, fear, shame, anger, confusion, trouble sleeping and concentrating. Some people feel “strangely calm”. All of this is the way the body copes.

## What helps afterwards

People who listen and do not blame you. Getting a sense of control back through small decisions. Therapy with a professional experienced in violence and trauma. Organisations that support survivors often offer free counselling, legal help and safe accommodation. Look for ones that are officially registered, and check their current contact details before relying on them.

If you know someone who has experienced violence: believe them, do not ask “why didn't you leave”, and do not push them to act before they are ready.
