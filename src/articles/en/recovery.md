People picture recovery as a staircase, each step higher than the last. In reality it is closer to a hike in the mountains: up, a bit down, up again, sometimes in the fog.

## What “better” means

It does not mean you forget, or that nothing touches you anymore. More often it means memories lose their sharpness, you can think about what happened without being flooded, your body calms down more easily, and you begin doing things you used to avoid. Or you return to people and activities you had withdrawn from.

There will be bad days afterwards too. That is not going back to zero, it is part of the process.

## What usually helps

- Safety and predictability in daily life.
- People you can reach out to without having to explain everything.
- The basics: sleep, food, movement. It is boring, but it matters.
- Trauma-focused therapy with a professional you trust.
- Patience with yourself, even when you think you “should be better by now”.

## How you know you are making progress

Not always through a big change. It may be that you fell asleep more easily today. That you laughed and did not feel guilty. That you asked for help. The small things are the path.

If you feel stuck, or things are getting worse, stay in touch with a professional. If you are in danger, call **112**.
