It usually starts with something that helps. A glass of wine to ease tension. A game to switch off. Something that, for a little while, quiets loneliness or pain. The trouble begins when what was once a help becomes a need, and stopping brings unpleasant feelings or strong cravings.

## How we tell something has gone out of control

There is no exact line, but these are often mentioned: use continues even though it is already causing harm; it is hard to stop or cut down as much as you decided; you spend a lot of time getting it or recovering; you start neglecting people and things that used to matter; you need more to get the same effect.

## Why it is not “weakness”

Substances and some behaviours change how the brain responds to pleasure and stress. Behind them there is often something else too: anxiety, depression, trauma. Shame and blame usually make things harder because they push people to hide.

## What helps

Support that does not judge you. A doctor and, especially with alcohol and some substances, medical supervision when stopping, because stopping abruptly can be dangerous. Therapy, mutual-support groups, support from people close to you.

If you feel you are losing control, you do not have to wait for it to “get worse”. Talking to your family doctor or an addiction specialist is a good first step. In a dangerous state, such as severe confusion or seizures, call **112**.
