In group therapy a few people (usually six to ten) meet regularly with one or two facilitators. Some groups have a shared theme, such as anxiety, loss or addiction, and others are more open.

## How a session goes

Usually between an hour and a half and two hours. The facilitator sets the frame: confidentiality, respect, the right to stay silent. Then each person shares if they wish and others respond. You do not need to say much at first. Many people begin by listening.

## What the group gives

The feeling that you are not the only one. That alone often brings relief. You hear how others cope. You can see how you behave with other people in real time and try new ways of relating in a safe setting. Many groups are also more affordable.

## What can be hard

It can be uncomfortable, and you will feel seen. Not everyone feels good in a group, and some difficulties, such as severe trauma or an acute crisis, may call for individual work first. It matters that the facilitator is experienced and the group is safe.

## What to ask

How many people are in it, what the rules are, whether you can try one session before committing, how confidentiality is protected.

No approach is best for everyone. What suits you depends on you, on the difficulty and on the person sitting across from you. That is why it is best to talk it through with a psychologist or psychotherapist.
