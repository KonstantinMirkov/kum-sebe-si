A traumatic experience is one in which you faced real or possible threat to your life, health or safety, or to someone close to you. It can be a single event, like a crash, an assault or a sudden loss, or something that repeats over a long time.

## Different people, different reactions

There is no “right” way here. Some people get better with time and the support of those around them. Others carry the effects for months and years. Some feel nothing at first and the reactions come much later. Even after the same event, two people can come out very differently, and that says nothing about their strength.

People most often describe:

- memories or nightmares that return uninvited;
- feeling constantly on guard, easily startled, struggling to fall asleep;
- avoiding places, people or conversations that remind them;
- guilt, shame, anger, or numbness instead;
- a harder time seeing the world as safe and people as trustworthy.

All of this can be an understandable reaction to an incomprehensible situation. Having these things does not automatically mean a particular diagnosis. Only a professional can assess that.

## When to get help

If a few weeks later the reactions are not easing, get in the way of daily life or are getting worse, there is no need to wait longer. There are therapies built specifically for trauma. If you have thoughts of harming yourself or are in danger, call **112**.
