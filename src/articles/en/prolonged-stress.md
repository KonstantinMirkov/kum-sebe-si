When we think of trauma, we usually picture one big event. But the body also wears down under a much quieter, continuous threat: years of bullying, living with a person who is unpredictable or dangerous, long-term care for someone seriously ill, war or insecurity, living somewhere you do not feel safe.

## What happens

The nervous system stays in “ready” mode. That is why people may feel constantly on edge, sleep badly, startle at small things, have physical complaints without a clear cause. It is also common to find it hard to relax even when nothing is threatening them, and then to feel guilty about it.

Long survival also changes how we see ourselves and others: harder trust, shame, a sense of being “broken”. That is not the truth about you, but a consequence of the circumstances.

## What helps

First, as far as possible, safety. It is very hard to recover while you are still in the same situation. Then: small, predictable routines, supportive people, limiting what keeps re-triggering the tension. And a professional with experience in the effects of prolonged traumatic experiences.

If you live with violence or fear for your safety, reach out for help straight away. In immediate danger, call **112**.
