Not every difficulty calls for long-term therapy. Sometimes you have a specific question or period: a decision you cannot make, a change in life, stress at work, a breakup, trouble with a child. Then counselling may be enough.

## What counselling is

A few sessions (sometimes 4 to 12) in which you talk with a trained professional, most often a psychologist. The aim is to look at the situation more clearly, sort out your thoughts and find a direction. You do not dig deep into the past unless there is a need.

## What psychoeducation is

It is simply explanation: how anxiety works, what happens in the body under stress, what we know about sleep, depression, trauma. It sounds small, but many people feel better when they learn that what is happening to them has an explanation and that they are not “going crazy”. It is often part of counselling or therapy, but you can also get it from good articles and books.

## When it fits

With a clearly defined problem, during life changes, as support through a hard period, as a first step when you are unsure whether you need therapy. It can help you decide what comes next.

## When it may not be enough

If difficulties are long-lasting, severe, recurring or there is danger, it is worth considering deeper therapy or a doctor.

No approach is best for everyone. What suits you depends on you, on the difficulty and on the person sitting across from you. That is why it is best to talk it through with a psychologist or psychotherapist.
