The hardest part is often booking the appointment. If you have done that, well done. Here is roughly what lies ahead.

## Before the meeting

You do not need to be prepared. But if it helps you feel calmer, jot down two or three things: what is troubling you, since when, what you have already tried. Think about what you would like to change, even if it is vague. You can write down questions too: how often will we meet, what does it cost, how is confidentiality protected.

## At the meeting itself

The first meeting is usually about getting acquainted. The professional will ask what brings you there, how your days go, what your relationships with those close to you are like, whether there is anything in your health they should know about. You do not have to tell everything the first time, and you can say “I am not ready to talk about that yet”. You may feel a little awkward, and that is completely normal.

## Afterwards

Ask yourself: did I feel heard? Did I feel respected? Do I have the sense that this person understands what I am telling them? You do not need to be sure after one meeting. But if something feels strongly wrong, you are not obliged to continue. A good relationship with the professional is one of the most important things in therapy.

## If it is urgent

If you are in immediate danger or thinking of harming yourself, do not wait for an appointment. Call **112**.
