We all get distracted or blow up now and then. But for some people, concentration is a constant battle, and emotions arrive with such force that they surprise even them.

## What may be behind it

Many things. Lack of sleep, stress, anxiety, depressive states, effects of difficult experiences, physical problems. For some people it is tied to the way their attention works, as in attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), which also occurs in adults and often goes unrecognised. On the other hand, harder emotional regulation also has many possible explanations.

That is why it does not pay to give yourself a label from an article. An assessment by a professional who looks at the whole picture is what is needed.

## What may help in the meantime

- Writing tasks down instead of holding them in your head.
- Short blocks of work with clear breaks.
- Fewer notifications, fewer tasks at once.
- With a strong emotion: give yourself a few minutes before reacting. It does not have to be the “right” reaction, just the slower one.
- Sleep, movement, regular meals.

## When to seek an assessment

If the difficulties have been there since childhood, get in the way of work, study or relationships, or wear you out. Start with your family doctor or a psychologist who can point you onward.
