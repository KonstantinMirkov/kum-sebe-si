Self-esteem is how the voice in your head sounds when you are alone. For some it is fair and even kind. For many people it sounds like a strict teacher commenting on everything: “messed up again”, “others are better”, “who are you kidding”.

## Where it comes from

Rarely from nowhere. The feedback we got as children, comparisons at home or at school, experiences of rejection, perfectionism all play a part. Social media adds a constant stream of other people's “best moments”. Low self-esteem is not a character flaw but a learned habit.

## What does not work

To someone who feels not good enough, “just love yourself” does not help. Empty affirmations you do not believe often irritate more than they soothe.

## What more often helps

- Noticing the critical voice and calling it a voice, not a fact.
- Asking yourself what you would say to a friend in the same position.
- Doing things you slowly get better at, without comparing yourself to others.
- Relationships in which you can be imperfect and still valued.

If self-criticism is constant, tied to sadness or to avoiding life, therapy can help, especially approaches that work with how you relate to yourself. Talk to a professional if you feel you cannot get out of this on your own.
