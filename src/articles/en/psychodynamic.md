Psychodynamic therapy comes from psychoanalysis but is more flexible and usually does not require several sessions a week. The idea is that part of how we react, love, avoid or defend ourselves has roots in early experience, and we are not always aware of it.

## How sessions go

The conversation is freer than in CBT. There is no set programme: you say what is on your mind, and the therapist helps you both notice recurring themes. For example how you behave in close relationships, what you feel towards authority, which dreams or memories keep returning. The relationship with the therapist is often observed too, because the same patterns as in your life sometimes appear in it. It can be short-term or run longer.

## What it is used for

People choose it when they repeat the same thing in different relationships, feel empty or inwardly conflicted, or want to understand themselves more deeply rather than only reduce a symptom. It is also used for depressive and anxious states.

## What is good to discuss

How much time you have, how often you can come, what you expect. Ask what training the therapist has.

No approach is best for everyone. What suits you depends on you, on the difficulty and on the person sitting across from you. That is why it is best to talk it through with a psychologist or psychotherapist.
