CBT is built on the idea that our thoughts, feelings, bodily sensations and behaviour are linked and can reinforce each other. For example: you think “I will fail”, you get anxious, you avoid the situation, and so you never find out whether it is true.

## What sessions look like

They are structured and goal-oriented. You and the therapist work out what you want to change, note what keeps the difficulty going and choose concrete steps. Between sessions there are often small “homework” exercises, such as observing the situations in which anxiety shows up or trying something you have been avoiding. It is usually time-limited: from a few weeks to a few months.

## What it is used for

It is applied to various difficulties, such as anxiety and panic attacks, depressive states, intrusive thoughts and sleep problems, and there is a trauma-focused version. For some conditions it is well studied.

## What you can discuss with the therapist

How many exercises are realistic for you, what is hard, how you will know it is working. CBT is not simply “think positive”. If that is how it sounds at the first session, say so.

No approach is best for everyone. What suits you depends on you, on the difficulty and on the person sitting across from you. That is why it is best to talk it through with a psychologist or psychotherapist.
