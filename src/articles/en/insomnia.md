Everyone has nights spent tossing and turning until two in the morning. That is not a problem. We talk about insomnia when trouble sleeping repeats on most nights, lasts for weeks and takes something from your days: you wake up wrecked, irritable, unable to focus.

## Why it happens

Usually there is no single cause. Stress and anxiety, low mood, an irregular routine, working late, screens, caffeine, alcohol, pain, certain medicines. And there is a trap: the more you worry about not falling asleep, the more awake your body gets.

## What often helps

- Roughly the same bedtime and wake-up time, weekends included.
- Keep the bed for sleep. If you are not asleep after about 20 minutes, get up, do something calm in dim light and go back when you feel sleepy.
- Coffee and energy drinks in the morning only. Alcohol helps you fall asleep but wrecks sleep later in the night.
- A little time before bed when you do not solve problems. Writing down what is bothering you can help it “stay” on the paper until morning.

## When to get help

If sleeplessness lasts more than a month, if you snore loudly and stop breathing in your sleep, or if it comes with sadness and anxiety, talk to your family doctor. There is a well-studied therapy made specifically for insomnia (CBT-I), and it is often recommended before sleeping pills.
