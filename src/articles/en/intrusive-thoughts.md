Nearly everyone has moments when something strange or frightening pops into their head: “what if I hit someone”, “did I lock the door”, “what if I get sick”. For most people such thoughts pass and are forgotten. For some, they stick and set off strong anxiety.

## A thought is not an action

An intrusive thought does not show who you are or what you will do. It is usually so upsetting precisely because it clashes with your values. The harder you try to push it away, the stronger it comes back.

## Compulsions

To bring the anxiety down, some people start doing things: checking several times, washing their hands, counting, repeating phrases, seeking reassurance. The relief is short and the cycle starts again, usually wider. When this takes a lot of time (more than about an hour a day), causes distress or gets in the way of life, we are talking about a condition called obsessive-compulsive disorder. Only a professional can judge whether that is your case.

## What helps

Cognitive behavioural therapy with a specific technique, in which you gradually and with support stop the compulsions, is well studied. It sounds scary, but it is done in small steps with a professional. For some people medication is added too.

If your thoughts frighten you or the repeated behaviour wears you out, you do not have to put up with it alone.
