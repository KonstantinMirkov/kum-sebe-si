Sadness comes and goes. It is a normal response to loss, disappointment or just a rough patch. Depressive states are something else: the sense of gloom or emptiness stays almost every day, lasts at least two weeks and starts to touch everything else.

## What people often describe

That nothing brings joy anymore, even things they used to love. That they are tired from the moment they wake up. That they sleep too much or too little. That concentrating or making even a small decision is hard. That they blame themselves for everything. Sometimes irritability or a numb feeling takes the place of sadness.

Not everyone has all of this, and not everyone looks “sad” from the outside. Many people keep going to work and smiling.

## What can help

Depression lies to you that nothing matters and nothing will change. So sometimes the first step is very small: stepping outside for ten minutes, answering a message, telling someone how you are. Regular sleep, daylight and movement have a real effect on mood, though they are rarely enough alone. Therapy and, when a doctor judges it right, medication help many people.

## If you are thinking about death

If you have thoughts of not wanting to live or of harming yourself, do not leave them spinning around alone. Tell someone you trust, or call **112** if you are in immediate danger. These thoughts are a sign that you need support, not proof that you are “weak”.
