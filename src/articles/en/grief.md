Grief is a response to loss: the death of someone close, a breakup, losing a job, your health, the plans you had. People expect it to look like sadness, but it is often messier. It can be anger, guilt, numbness, or relief that then makes you feel guilty. You may feel nothing for the first few days and then get hit out of nowhere.

## There is no schedule

The popular idea of the “stages of grief” has helped many people feel understood, but it is not a timetable. You do not pass through them in order, and nobody is timing you. Some days are lighter, then a wave comes back, on a birthday, say, or from a familiar smell. That is part of the process, not a sign that you have “gone backwards”.

## What sometimes helps

- Talking about the person or the loss, if you have someone to talk to.
- Small routines: sleep, food, a walk, even when you do not feel like it.
- Not making big decisions right away, if they can wait.
- Rituals that matter to you, even simple ones.

## When to reach out

If many months later the pain is just as strong and keeps you away from life, if you have thoughts of harming yourself or feel you cannot function, talk to a professional. There is also what is called prolonged grief, where support can make a real difference.
