A boundary is simply a line between what you are okay with and what you are not. How much time you give people, what you allow to be said to you, how far the help you offer goes. When boundaries are blurry, one of two things tends to happen: you dissolve into other people, or you retreat behind a wall.

## What it looks like in practice

You say “yes” when you mean “no” and then quietly resent it. You take on other people's problems. You are afraid of letting anyone down. Or the reverse: when someone gets close, you pull away, you do not ask for help, you do not show what is hard for you.

These habits usually have a history. They may have been learned in a family where one person's needs outweighed everyone else's, or where voicing an opinion was risky. They were a sensible way to cope back then. Now they just no longer serve you.

## Small steps

Pause for a second before you answer. “Let me think and I will get back to you” is a perfectly honest answer. Try it first in low-stakes situations. Notice how you feel afterwards; guilt usually comes first and slowly fades.

## When therapy can help

If you keep repeating the same pattern across different relationships, feel trapped, or are afraid of people close to you, talking to a professional can be useful. If you fear a partner or parent, call **112** in immediate danger.
